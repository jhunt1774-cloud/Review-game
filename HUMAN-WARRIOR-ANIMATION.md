# Human warrior combat animation

This branch verifies attack timing using the retired Alden artwork. The art is unsuitable for the painted roster. Keep this branch as a mechanics reference until a painted Human warrior is ready.

## Play it

Start a quest or visit camp and select **Alden · Human warrior** as the vanguard appearance. You can also select Alden as a vanguard companion while playing another role. In battle, select Alden, choose the ordinary attack and answer correctly. Alden uses the existing vanguard stats, damage type, focus rules, equipment slots and quiz flow. Thorin stays the default for new adventures and older saves.

The sword clip uses Alden's standard sword and shield. With loot gear equipped, the attack uses the existing gear-aware poses so the displayed weapon and armor match the loadout. Skills, including Sundering axe and Whirlwind cleave, still use their existing rules, names and presentation paths. Still poses and an unavailable sheet use the existing basic attack presentation.

## Renderer and asset audit

- The repository-root `index.html` is the current game. The `forest-path/` folder and standalone HTML are legacy versions.
- `party-upgrade.js` selects painted character atlases by roster ID. `characters.js` crops one frame at a time into a portrait canvas. `equipment-appearance.js` prebakes visible gear onto the registered body frames. The starter warrior (Thorin) is a dwarf with an axe. Iden and Eldric are the Human ranger and wizard.
- The former basic melee presentation slides the whole character forward, plays a handful of prepainted poses and retreats. Some skills also rotate the whole portrait. The enemy renderer already has hurt and defeat states. The battle damage callback owns HP, tactical and equipment effects, and starts both those reactions and the shared contact effects.
- Legacy character and encounter art is tracked in the asset manifest and prompt files. Those files record selected sources and generation history. A single repository-wide art license was not found. Verify redistribution rights per asset before reusing the legacy art outside this game.
- Alden's art, shield, blade trail and attack poses are original procedural artwork authored in `tools/warrior-art-source.js`. They use no external textures, fonts or character art. The unchanged sword sounds are the credited Still North Media Sabre Swing, Vehicle/Jan Schupke metal impacts and Kenney soft impacts. Their CC0 provenance and exact crops are in [AUDIO-CREDITS.txt](AUDIO-CREDITS.txt). Other generated effects and music have their own credit records.

## Author and extend

1. Draw a clean full-body silhouette with a consistent ground line, hand grips, facing and scale. Keep leg, hip, shoulder, wrist and weapon motion in the source. Alden uses a 240 × 240 cell at a shared ground line of y=220. The guard at the beginning and end matches the idle drawing.
2. Author named beats and test the silhouette at battle size. Alden closes long gaps with animated strides, then gathers weight during the opening 256 ms of the swing, releases on frame 8, connects on frame 10 at 320 ms and returns to guard by 736 ms before stepping back. The contact cell samples the fully extended blade pose. The transparent blade trail is baked into the swing cells.
3. Run `node tools/bake-warrior.cjs` using desktop Chrome. Set `CHROME_PATH` for a different installation. The script writes five transparent WebP atlases to `assets/warrior/`. Battle and travel have separate weaponless sheets for equipment composition. The 1200 × 1440 attack atlas uses 23 swing cells and four stride cells in a 5 × 6 grid. Bump the versioned URLs in `human-sword-attack.js` when replacing released files.
4. Preload only for the eligible fighter. Copy the active cell into one 240 × 240 overlay canvas. Keep wind-up, swing, contact, and recovery on the same timeline. Use the existing hit callback once at contact so recoil, HP, damage text, sparks and audio agree. Projectiles should call the same callback when they reach the target. In an enemy clip, preserve the defense controller's release window and call its existing impact callback.
5. For a Dwarf hammer or axe, author shorter planted steps and a contact pose that matches the head of the weapon. For an Elf bow, place the release marker where the string leaves the fingers. For a Wizard, keep the spell origin on the hand or staff. For enemies, build readable wind-ups and separate hurt/defeat states. Keep current fallback poses, score logic and defense timings while replacing individual clips.
6. Test wrong answers, correct and lethal hits, weapon swaps, still poses, a blocked atlas request, repeat attacks, mobile layout and sound off/on. On a school Chromebook, compare first and repeat strikes against Thorin and look for pauses before contact. This animation adds a a roughly 164 KiB WebP download and one decoded 1200 × 1440 sheet (about 6.6 MiB at four bytes per pixel). It uses a single 240 × 240 overlay, with a source rectangle copied at most once per 32 ms pose interval. No skeletal calculations or pixel reads run during a strike.

## Review and rollout

1. **Finish Alden first.** The geometric prototype art differs from the painted roster. Keep the attack timing and pose landmarks while bringing the silhouette, lighting and materials into the same style. On a school Chromebook, compare first and repeat strikes with Thorin at 1024 × 600 and 1366 × 768. Check playback with sound enabled and muted, and check Still poses and the gear fallback. See [CHROMEBOOK-TRIAL.md](CHROMEBOOK-TRIAL.md).
2. **Extend one move per hero, in order.** Start with Thorin's axe and reuse the melee contact marker. Add the ranger's bow next, with separate string-release and projectile-impact markers. Then add the wizard's cast with a readable charge and spell origin. Give the defender a distinct planted stance and shield recovery. Verify that threat, defense prompts and equipped gear still read correctly in each matchup.
3. **Polish the first Wildwood loop.** Play camp → first fight → reward → next trail objective in a classroom-length session. Prioritize clearer enemy windups, short post-answer downtime, and an obvious route to the next objective. Verify save and resume at each of those transitions.
4. **Tighten the teacher workflow.** The existing workspace already has saved sets, class folders, teacher playtests, backups and anonymous report imports. Prioritize a preview of question counts and rejected rows before an import is applied. Then improve the class results view with low-sample indicators and a downloadable summary by standard. Test duplicate report imports and backup restoration with the existing teacher tooling.
5. **Review release assets.** Maintain a source and license record for each shipped art and audio asset. The shipped document readers have individual license files. Check legacy art and generated music against their source records before expanding distribution.
